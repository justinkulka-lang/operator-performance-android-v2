1 Authority Scope and Supersession

This document is the sole active ARES authority. Earlier ARES authorities and instructions are historical and cannot override it.

No training beyond Day 224 is created. A continuation requires a separately approved authority.

Medical restrictions and injury management override participation. This authority does not provide diagnosis, clearance, or rehabilitation.

The program is evidence-informed but cannot guarantee simultaneous biological maxima in hypertrophy and running performance. It uses a hypertrophy-prioritized hybrid compromise with explicit recovery controls.

App implementation must reproduce the prescriptions exactly. Presentation changes cannot silently alter training.

2 Program Architecture

Four eight-week phases use a fixed seven-day week. Engine work is performed after the weekly rest, Speed receives a separate quality day, easy aerobic work follows Upper B, and the two lower sessions remain separated from Speed by approximately 48 hours.

| Phase | Weeks | Emphasis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Foundation | 1–8 | Hypertrophy base, running tolerance, acceleration, aerobic foundation |
| 2 Development | 9–16 | Strength, maximum velocity, threshold and interval development |
| 3 Specific Development | 17–24 | Special endurance and 1.5-mile goal-pace development while retaining hypertrophy volume |
| 4 Integration and Peak | 25–32 | Event specificity, controlled fatigue reduction, and final tests |

3 Global Training Rules

Only hard working sets count. Warm-up and ramp sets do not count.

Primary compound lifts stop at the prescribed RIR and never reach uncontrolled failure. Lower-body isolation retains at least 1 RIR. Upper-body isolation may reach 0–1 RIR only as prescribed.

Use a full controlled range of motion that remains pain-free and technically stable.

Use double progression. Add repetitions within the listed range. When every working set reaches the top of the range at the prescribed RIR, increase load by the smallest practical increment and return to the bottom of the range.

Default load increases are approximately 5 lb for upper-body exercises and 5–10 lb for lower-body exercises. Smaller increases are permitted.

Rest 2–4 minutes for compounds and 1–2 minutes for isolation work. Prescribed sprint and interval recoveries take precedence.

No rucking, loaded carries, sled work, unauthorized finishers, extra intervals, extra lifting, or extra plyometrics.

An exercise alternative replaces the listed movement; it is never added to it.

3.1 RIR Calibration and Progression

RIR means the number of additional technically acceptable repetitions that could have been completed with the same range and execution.

Every fourth normal week, the final set of one safe upper-body isolation and one safe lower-body isolation may continue to technical failure solely to compare actual repetitions with the predicted RIR. This calibration set replaces the prescribed final set; it is not an extra set.

If the calibration result exceeds the prediction by more than two repetitions, increase the next working load or correct the RIR estimate.

One weak session does not change the plan. A greater than 5% decline in the same movement for two consecutive exposures permits removal of one set from that movement for the next exposure. Restore it when performance returns.

Unintentional body-mass loss across two weeks together with regression in multiple lifts requires correction of energy intake and recovery before further lifting volume is removed.

| Cooldown | After Engine or Speed, walk or jog very easily for 8–10 min. Do not add strides, intervals, or conditioning. |

3.3 Sprint and Plyometric Rules

All Speed work is actual running on a safe, predictable surface. Treadmill sprinting is not the default.

Build-ups and drill distances do not count toward quality-sprint volume. Prescribed quality volume may not exceed 600 m. A 600 m session displays a workload warning, not a claim of a validated injury threshold.

A timed 300 m is permitted only after two pain-free running exposures in the preceding 14 days, including one exposure reaching about 90% of current perceived top speed without altered gait or next-day symptom escalation. If the gate is not met, perform 6 x 30 m progressive accelerations at 70–85% with 3 min recovery and classify the test as MISSING.

Weeks 3–7, 9–15, 17–23, and 25–29 begin Speed with 3 x 3 pogo jumps and 3 x 2 standing broad jumps after the running warm-up. Broad jumps remain at 70–80% of the best comfortable distance. Rest 60–90 s and stop for pain or declining landing quality.

Acceleration and flying repetitions use technical intent, not split times derived from a 300 m result. Accelerate smoothly, remain relaxed, and stop before straining.

Stop Speed work for altered gait, sharp pain, or clear mechanical deterioration. Do not replace stopped repetitions.

5 Exact Conditioning Progression

Day 1 Engine occurs before short upper. Day 4 is Speed. Day 5 easy aerobic occurs after Upper B. The app displays the entire required sequence, but it does not require result entry.

5.1 Intensity and Execution Definitions

| Term | Controlling definition |
| --- | --- |
| Easy aerobic | RPE 2–3 of 10. Full-sentence conversation remains comfortable. Run on a flat predictable route or use the prescribed bike option. |
| Threshold | RPE 7–8. Controlled discomfort with speech limited to short phrases. Pace remains even and never becomes a time trial. |
| Controlled interval | RPE 8 with even repetitions. The last repetition must remain mechanically clean and no slower than the first by more than 3%. |
| Goal pace | Exact 10:00 1.5-mile reference pace. If it cannot be repeated inside the 3% slowdown rule, use the fastest repeatable clean pace for that session and record the prescribed session as not attained outside the app. |
| Speed percentage | Percentage of current pain-free 300 m average velocity for 150–300 m repetitions only. Target time = valid 300 m time x distance / 300 / stated decimal percentage. |
| Acceleration or fly | Technique-led. No mandatory split. Full recovery means begin only when breathing is controlled and the athlete can reproduce relaxed mechanics. |
| Recovery | Easy means walking or very slow jogging. Do not turn recoveries into additional tempo work. |
| Termination | Stop the quality portion for altered gait, pain, mechanical breakdown, or a repetition slower than the first by more than 3%. Do not add replacement repetitions. |

Every Engine quality session uses the Engine warm-up, the listed work and recovery, then an 8–10 min easy cooldown. Every Speed session uses the Speed warm-up, listed plyometrics when authorized, listed repetitions, full prescribed recovery, then an 8–10 min cooldown. Two consecutive unattained quality sessions require review before the next quality exposure.

7 Recovery Substitution and Adjustment Rules

Planned consolidation and taper weeks are mandatory parts of the program.

When a movement causes pain or cannot be performed safely, use the first available ranked alternative for the same movement pattern with the same sets, repetitions, and RIR.

A missed day pauses the sequence. Resume with that same program day when training can safely continue. Do not skip forward, double sessions, or remove Day 7 rest.

Easy aerobic work must remain conversational and cannot become an unlisted tempo session.

If a goal-pace workout is unattainable, complete only the prescribed number of repetitions at the fastest clean repeatable pace. Do not add repetitions or retest.

Pain that changes running gait or lifting mechanics ends that exercise. Persistent symptoms require qualified evaluation before progression.

Performance results remain external to the app.

8 Coaching and Athlete Support Authority

ARES coaching helps the athlete execute this fixed program. It may individualize nutrition, hydration, sleep, environment, supplementation, and practical scheduling. It may not silently rewrite training. Dynamic personal information remains external to the immutable authority and outside the Android app.

For a today question, resolve the exact authority version, week, day, phase, and overlay, then present the complete session.

Nutrition guidance supports adequate energy availability, approximately 1.6–2.2 g/kg/day protein unless medical guidance requires otherwise, sufficient carbohydrate for quality training, and individualized hydration.

Wearable scores, one poor session, one weigh-in, or ordinary low motivation do not authorize program changes.

Chest pain, fainting, unexplained severe breathlessness, acute neurologic symptoms, severe heat illness, or suspected serious injury stops training and requires appropriate evaluation.
